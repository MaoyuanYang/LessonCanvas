Planning a Meaningful Journey

More and more senior-high students see travel as a classroom without walls. Before packing a bag, careful travellers ask three questions: where to go, how to get there, and what to learn along the way.

Consider a trip along the Yangtze River. A well-planned route might start in Chongqing, pass the Three Gorges by overnight boat, and end in Nanjing. Each stop offers a different lesson: the mountain city's geography, the river canyons' role in Chinese poetry, and the busy ports that connect inland industry with the sea.

Budgeting is the next skill. Comparing train, coach, and air ticket prices teaches students to balance time against money. A night train costs less than a flight and saves one night's hotel fee, while a slow boat turns the journey itself into the destination.

Finally, good travellers prepare for the unexpected. They check the weather forecast, keep copies of important documents, and learn a few phrases of the local language. When a plan changes, they treat the change as a new page in their travel story rather than a failure.

Travel, planned thoughtfully, is not an escape from study. It is study — geography, history, economics, and language walking side by side.
